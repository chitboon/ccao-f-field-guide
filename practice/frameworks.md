# Practice · Anthropic's Frameworks

**22 items.** Framework-recall questions in the "which is most descriptive of X" style. This item type is heavily represented on the exam and almost absent from other prep material.

**Why this set exists.** These are not judgment questions. You either know which framework component owns which idea, or you guess. And the distractors are usually *correct definitions of a neighbouring component* — so partial knowledge reads as confident wrongness.

> **Calibration note.** One candidate's recollection of a live sitting is that framework items were pitched at the level of the **four Ds themselves** — asking which option best describes a named D — rather than at sub-component level. That is a single fallible recollection, not data, so items at both levels are kept here. But if you are short of time, **prioritise items 1 to 8**: getting the four Ds crisply apart is the higher-value skill, and sub-component knowledge mostly earns its keep by making the top-level calls reliable.

**How to use it.** Answer before opening the reveal. Log misses by trap family, not by item number.

**1.** `[D-def]` Which best describes **Delegation**?

A. Communicating your requirements clearly and completely enough for the AI to act on them without guessing.
B. Taking responsibility for the AI-assisted work you create, disclose, and share onward to others.
C. Evaluating what the AI produced before you rely on it for anything.
D. Judging which parts of a piece of work should go to AI and which you keep, then splitting them.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Every distractor is an accurate definition of a *different* B — A is Description, C is Discernment, B is Diligence. That is the standard construction for this item type, so the skill is knowing which B owns which verb: **Delegation decides, Description communicates, Discernment evaluates, Diligence takes responsibility.**
</details>

**2.** `[D-def]` Which best describes **Description**?

A. Judging whether what came back is accurate, complete and fit for its purpose.
B. Communicating what you want clearly, including how the AI should approach and behave.
C. Working out which parts of the job are worth handing to an AI in the first place.
D. Owning the result, including disclosing how it was made and vouching for it.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Description is the competency of communicating clearly with an AI system, and note that it covers more than the request itself — it includes how the AI should approach the task and how it should behave with you. A is Discernment, C is Delegation, D is Diligence. **Memorise the four verbs: Delegation decides, Description communicates, Discernment evaluates, Diligence takes responsibility.**
</details>

**3.** `[D-def]` Which best describes **Discernment**?

A. Specifying the output, the method and the manner you want from the AI.
B. Deciding which work to keep for yourself and which to hand across.
C. Evaluating the AI's output, its process, and its behaviour with a critical eye.
D. Being transparent about AI's role and accountable for what you circulate.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Discernment is critical evaluation — and note it evaluates three things, not just the output: the product, the process that produced it, and the AI's behaviour during the exchange. D is the closest distractor, because owning what you circulate *feels* like evaluation; but disclosure and accountability are Diligence.
</details>

**4.** `[D-def]` Which best describes **Diligence**?

A. Ensuring your AI collaboration is responsible, transparent and accountable.
B. Checking the output carefully before you decide whether to rely on it.
C. Giving the AI enough context and constraint to produce what you need.
D. Assessing which tasks suit AI collaboration and which do not.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Diligence is the competency of engaging with AI responsibly. Academy draws a sharp line worth remembering: Delegation, Description and Discernment address **effectiveness and efficiency**, while Diligence alone addresses **ethics and safety**. B is Discernment — careful checking is evaluation, not accountability.
</details>

**5.** `[D-def]` An associate reviews a draft market analysis, notices a figure she cannot trace to any source, and removes it. Which competency is she exercising?

A. Delegation
B. Description
C. Diligence
D. Discernment

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Evaluating the output and acting on that evaluation is Discernment. C is the trap and a good one: *verifying before circulating* would be Deployment Diligence, but here she is assessing a draft in progress rather than vouching for something she is about to share. **The discriminator is assessing versus vouching.**
</details>

**6.** `[D-def]` An associate decides that a regulatory filing must be owned by the legal team from the outset rather than drafted first and reviewed afterwards. Which competency is this primarily?

A. Delegation
B. Discernment
C. Description
D. Diligence

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** This is a decision about what work to take on at all and who should own it — Delegation. D is highly defensible and worth thinking through, since the reasoning is plainly about responsibility; but Diligence governs how you handle work you *are* doing, whereas the call here is whether to take it on. Items like this are why the four verbs matter more than the four labels.
</details>

**7.** `[D-def]` A team gets fluent, well-structured output and circulates it without checking the figures. Which competency did they skip?

A. Delegation, since the task should not have been handed over.
B. Description, since the prompt did not ask for accuracy.
C. Discernment, since fluent output was treated as verified output.
D. Diligence, since no disclosure statement was attached.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Fluency being mistaken for accuracy is the canonical Discernment failure — and it has a mechanism behind it, since next token prediction produces the fluency and the fabrication from the same process. B misdiagnoses it: no prompt makes accuracy automatic. D names a real obligation that is not the one skipped here.
</details>

**8.** `[D-sub]` An associate lists what the AI can and cannot do on their organisation's plan before deciding which parts of a report to hand over. Which component is this?

A. Problem Awareness
B. Task Delegation
C. Platform Awareness
D. Process Description

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Platform Awareness is knowing what the available systems are capable of. Problem Awareness (A) is grasping the goal and the shape of the work — that comes first. Task Delegation (B) is the dividing itself, which comes after both awarenesses. D belongs to a different D entirely.
</details>

**9.** `[D-sub]` Which is the clearest example of **Process Description**?

A. "Produce a two-page brief containing an executive summary and three clear recommendations."
B. "Work through the regional figures first, then look for the pattern across them."
C. "Be concise and challenge my assumptions rather than agreeing with me."
D. "The audience is the board of directors, and the decision it must support is whether to renew the contract."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Process Description guides *how* the AI should approach the task. A is Product Description (what to create), C is Performance Description (how to behave), D is context that supports Product Description. All four are good prompt content — only one is Process.
</details>

**10.** `[D-sub]` Which is **Performance Description**?

A. Specifying the format and length of the deliverable.
B. Specifying the particular steps, method or framework the AI ought to follow in its approach.
C. Specifying how the AI should behave with you during the collaboration.
D. Specifying the criteria by which the finished output will be measured once it has been delivered.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Product is *what*, Process is *how it works*, Performance is *how it behaves*. D is the plausible trap — it sounds like performance in the workplace-review sense, but measuring output after delivery is evaluation, which belongs to Discernment.
</details>

**11.** `[D-sub]` An associate notices the AI reached the right conclusion but got there by ignoring one of the three data sources provided. Which type of Discernment is this?

A. Process Discernment
B. Product Discernment
C. Performance Discernment
D. Deployment Diligence

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Process Discernment assesses *how* the output was arrived at — whether the reasoning holds, whether anything was overlooked, whether the approach was appropriate. The output itself was fine, so it is not Product Discernment. Note that a right answer reached wrongly is still a finding: the next similar task may not survive the same gap.
</details>

**12.** `[D-sub]` Which is the clearest example of **Creation Diligence**?

A. Verifying a statistic against its original source document before the report is circulated onward.
B. Telling a client, when they ask directly, that AI assisted in preparing their deliverable.
C. Choosing an approved enterprise tool rather than a personal account for client data.
D. Deciding a task needs human expertise and keeping it in-house.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Creation Diligence is about the judgement in choosing a tool and deciding how you will work with it — tool choice, what data you share, the privacy and security considerations in that decision. **It is not carefulness during the making,** which is the natural misreading of the name. A is Deployment Diligence, B is Transparency Diligence, D is Task Delegation.
</details>

**13.** `[D-sub]` Where does **verification before sharing** sit in the framework?

A. Product Discernment, since verifying a claim is itself a form of evaluating the output.
B. Deployment Diligence, since it is taking responsibility for what you share.
C. Transparency Diligence, since verifying a claim is part of disclosing your work honestly.
D. Performance Discernment, since it assesses the AI's reliability.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Deployment Diligence is defined as taking responsibility for what you use or pass on — checking it, and then standing behind it. A is the close call and worth understanding: evaluating a claim *mid-draft* is Product Discernment, while verifying it *before it goes out* is Deployment Diligence. The discriminator is whether you are **assessing** or **vouching**.
</details>

**14.** `[loop]` The Description–Discernment loop has four steps. Which is the fourth?

A. Integrate — add your own expertise and take responsibility for the result.
B. Discern — evaluate the output itself, the process behind it, and the behaviour shown.
C. Refine — adjust the description based on what you found.
D. Describe — state the product, the process and the performance behaviour you want.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The loop runs Describe → Discern → Refine → **Integrate**. Most accounts of it stop at Refine, which omits the step that actually closes it: deciding what survives, what changes, what gets discarded, and owning the result. Integrate is where the loop hands off to Diligence.
</details>

**15.** `[loop]` An associate reviews a draft, identifies that it buries the recommendation, and rewrites the prompt to lead with the decision. Which does this illustrate?

A. Task Delegation, since work is being reassigned.
B. Creation Diligence, since the associate is taking care over the artefact being produced.
C. Platform Awareness, since the associate is working within the limits of the tool available.
D. The Description–Discernment loop, since evaluation is feeding a revised description.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** This is the loop by definition — discernment producing a better description. B is the trap, because "improving the work" sounds like diligence; Creation Diligence is about which system you chose and what you shared with it, not about the iteration mechanic.
</details>

**16.** `[mode]` An associate configures a workspace with standing instructions and reference files so a weekly report can be produced without re-explaining the context each time. Which engagement mode is this?

A. Agency
B. Augmentation
C. Automation
D. Delegation

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Agency is defined as configuring the system to act on your behalf, establishing what it knows and how it behaves rather than handing it individual tasks. Setting up standing context *is* Agency mode. C is task-by-task execution to instruction, B is collaborating turn by turn. D is not a mode at all — it is one of the 4Ds, which is the trap.
</details>

**17.** `[mode]` Which correctly pairs a mode with its definition?

A. Augmentation — the AI simply completes the specific tasks you set out in your instructions.
B. Augmentation — you and the AI think through the problem and do the work together.
C. Agency — you and the AI work through a problem together, turn by turn.
D. Automation — you and the AI collaborate together as creative thinking and execution partners.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The three modes: **Automation** — the AI completes specific tasks to your instructions. **Augmentation** — you collaborate as partners. **Agency** — you configure it to work independently, shaping knowledge and behaviour. A and D swap the first two; C describes Augmentation while labelling it Agency.
</details>

**18.** `[prop]` Which property explains why a model produces a confident, precisely-formatted citation that does not exist?

A. Knowledge, because the citation was outside the training data.
B. Next token prediction, because it generates plausible continuations rather than retrieving facts.
C. Working memory, because the source was pushed out of context.
D. Steerability, because the instruction to cite accurately was given and then not followed properly.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Next token prediction is the mechanism: the model builds an answer one piece at a time from what usually follows what, so a plausible-looking citation is a plausible continuation, not a retrieval failure. A is the close call — and in practice hallucinated specifics are usually **next token prediction + knowledge** interacting, one of the two named diagnostic pairs. But the fabrication itself comes from the generative mechanism.
</details>

---

**19.** `[prop]` Which property fails at an edge rather than degrading gradually?

A. Next token prediction
B. Steerability
C. Knowledge
D. Working memory

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Working memory: material either fits the context window or it does not, and the failure mode is silent truncation, with no guarantee of a warning. The other three degrade gradually — knowledge thins out, steerability drifts, prediction gets less reliable in sparse territory. Only working memory drops off an edge.
</details>

**20.** `[prop]` Which pairs a property with the competency it most directly supports?

A. Knowledge → Discernment
B. Steerability → Diligence
C. Knowledge → Delegation
D. Next token prediction → Description

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** Unevenness in what the model knows is what makes this a Delegation question — knowing where its coverage is thick and where it is thin is what tells you when to hand a task over and when to supply the context yourself. The documented links are: next token prediction → Discernment, knowledge → Delegation, working memory → Description, steerability → Description.
</details>

**21.** `[fp]` Fine-tuning leaves four characteristic marks on behaviour. Which is **not** one of them?

A. A pull toward sycophancy.
B. A tendency to truncate long inputs silently.
C. A default toward verbosity.
D. A gap between how confident it sounds and how reliable it actually is.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Silent truncation is a working-memory limitation, not a fine-tuning fingerprint. The four fingerprints are sycophancy, verbosity, reflexive hedging, and confidence that does not track reliability — all consequences of training on human preference judgments. This item tests the boundary between the two frameworks.
</details>

**22.** `[fp]` Why is asking the model to rate its own confidence unreliable?

A. The model has no access to its own reasoning process.
B. Confidence ratings are only meaningful when web search is enabled.
C. Stated confidence is loosely calibrated against actual reliability, as a fingerprint of fine-tuning.
D. The model will always report high confidence regardless of the question it has been asked.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** A gap between expressed confidence and actual reliability is a named fingerprint of fine-tuning. Confidence is a trained-in conversational behaviour, not a measurement — so a high rating carries no information about accuracy. D overstates it: the ratings are not uniformly high, they are simply uninformative.
</details>

**23.** `[fp]` An associate asks for feedback on a strategy and prefaces it with "I think this is bulletproof." The response is broadly supportive. What is the most likely explanation, and the appropriate response?

A. The strategy is sound, and the supportive response simply confirms as much.
B. Sycophancy — re-ask with an explicit invitation to disagree.
C. Over-caution — the model avoided disagreeing to stay safe.
D. Verbosity — the response padded agreement into length.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Sycophancy is the pull toward validating your framing, and it comes from fine-tuning on human preferences — humans prefer being agreed with. The documented countermeasure is an explicit invitation — a standing request, put in the prompt, to be contradicted when you are wrong. Note this is a **Description** move, not a Discernment one — you pre-authorise dissent rather than catching flattery afterwards. C is the near-miss: over-caution is reflexive hedging, not agreement.
</details>

**24.** `[cross]` A summary of a short document runs to three pages when one would do. What kind of failure is this?

A. A steerability limitation, since the length instruction was not followed.
B. A knowledge limitation, since the model had no sense of what length would be appropriate here.
C. A working memory limitation, since the model over-attended to the input.
D. A fine-tuning fingerprint, addressed in the prompt rather than by changing tools.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Verbosity is a trained-in default, not a capability gap. This distinction matters because **a fingerprint failure looks like a capability failure and is not one** — so the fix is a prompt-level constraint, not a different tier, tool, or model. A would be correct only if a specific length had been stated and then ignored.
</details>

**25.** `[cross]` Which describes the relationship between Description and Discernment?

A. Discernment is the flip side of Description, and its three types mirror the three Descriptions.
B. They are independent competencies applied at different project stages.
C. Discernment necessarily precedes Description, since you must settle your criteria before asking for anything.
D. Description handles the prompt and Discernment handles the configuration.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Academy presents Discernment as the mirror image of Description, and the symmetry is exact: Product, Process and Performance Discernment evaluate precisely the three things Product, Process and Performance Description specify. That mirroring is what makes the loop work.
</details>

**26.** `[cross]` Which item belongs at the "lasting" end of the Description spectrum?

A. Setting standing instructions that apply to every future session.
B. Rephrasing a request after receiving an unsatisfactory first answer.
C. Attaching an example of the output format you want.
D. Uploading a reference file to the current conversation.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** The spectrum is organised by durability: basic is single-turn shaping (iterating, adding context, uploading a file), middle has moderate persistence, and lasting is configuration affecting all subsequent interactions. Note it sits under *Description* — choosing where a rule lives is a description decision at a longer time horizon.
</details>

**27.** `[D-sub]` What is a diligence statement?

A. An acknowledgment of AI's role in the work, together with your assumption of responsibility for the output.
B. A log of every prompt used in producing a piece of work.
C. A description of the model's training data and known limitations.
D. A notice recording which model version and which capability tier produced the output in question.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** It does two jobs at once, which is the point: it discloses which systems assisted and with what tasks, *and* affirms that the output was reviewed and that the content, its accuracy and its presentation remain your responsibility. B statement that only discloses, or only affirms, is not one.
</details>

**28.** `[cross]` AI Fluency is defined by four adjectives: working with AI effectively, efficiently, ethically and safely. Which competency carries the last two?

A. Delegation, since deciding what is appropriate to hand over is itself an ethical judgement.
B. Discernment, since it catches output that would cause harm.
C. All four equally, since ethics is a cross-cutting concern.
D. Diligence, which addresses ethics and safety while the other three address effectiveness and efficiency.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**D.** Academy draws this line explicitly: the first three competencies are about working effectively and efficiently, while Diligence is the one carrying ethics and safety. C is attractive and wrong — ethics being *important* everywhere is not the same as it being *allocated* everywhere, and the framework allocates it.
</details>
